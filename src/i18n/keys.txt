Cruise regions of the world
Every region has its best season. Use this guide to choose where and when to sail.
Caribbean and Bahamas
Best season: December to April
Beaches, coral reefs and many islands on one trip. The hurricane season runs from June to November.
Mediterranean
Best season: May to October
Ancient cities, Greek islands, Italian and Spanish ports, and great food.
Northern Europe and Norwegian fjords
Best season: May to September
Fjords, glaciers and Baltic capitals, with very long summer days.
Alaska
Glaciers, whales, bears and small towns surrounded by mountains.
Asia and Japan
Best season: spring and autumn in Japan
Temples, modern cities and street food. The best months change from country to country.
Australia and South Pacific
Best season: November to March
Coral reefs, white beaches and Pacific islands such as Fiji and Tahiti.
South America and Antarctica
Patagonia, Cape Horn, Rio de Janeiro and expedition cruises to Antarctica.
Hawaii
Best season: all year
Volcanoes, beaches and a different island almost every day.
River cruises
Europe: spring to autumn. Nile: October to April
Small ships on the Rhine, the Danube, the Nile and the Mekong, with a new town almost every day.
World cruises
Most start in January
Several continents on one long voyage. Some cruise lines also sell shorter parts of the trip.

Cruise guides
Simple tips to plan your cruise with confidence.
Your first cruise: what to know
Book early to get more cabin choices.
Cruise prices are usually per person, based on two people in one cabin.
Check what is included. Drinks, Wi-Fi, tips and excursions are often extra.
Arrive in the port city one day before the cruise starts.
Keep your passport and medicines in your hand luggage on boarding day.
How to choose a cabin
Inside cabins are the cheapest and have no window.
Ocean view cabins have a window that does not open.
Balcony cabins have a private outdoor space.
Suites are larger and often include extra services.
Cabins in the middle of the ship and on lower decks feel less movement.
What to pack
Passport and travel documents, plus a printed copy.
Sunscreen, sunglasses and a hat.
A light jacket for windy evenings on deck.
One elegant outfit for special dinners.
Check the list of items your cruise line does not allow on board.
Port days
Read the "all aboard" time every morning. The ship does not wait for late passengers.
Excursions made for cruise passengers usually promise to bring you back on time.
Carry the phone number of the ship's port agent.
Use local money or a card that has no foreign transaction fees.
Passports and visas
Many countries ask for a passport that is valid for at least six months after your trip.
Check the visa rules for every country on your route, not only the first port.
Rules depend on your nationality, so check with official government sources.
Embassies and travel advice
Before you sail, check entry rules and safety advice from official sources.
